Design notes

Why the robot is built the way it is. Not needed at the bench — useful for your report, and for anyone changing the design.

Why an FOC ESC

Several ESC solutions were explored for the FAAV concept:

  • Regular drone ESCs. Hobbyking 12A and EP Aer11 were tested. Both start losing synchronisation at low speed, which is a problem for water propulsion — precisely the regime the robot spends most of its time in.
  • FOC ESCs tend to be more complex and larger. The G431-ESC1 board is favourable and had already been explored in the lab.

That tradeoff — accepting a larger, more complex driver to get usable low-speed torque control — is the reason Session 3 spends a whole session on the driver.

Speed ceiling and sensor lag

Maximum speed went from 500 to 1700 rad/s, enough to drive the wings at 10 Hz, through a combination of sensor and MCU changes documented in Session 4. The remaining constraint is sensor lag: above 500–600 rad/s, uncompensated lag shows up as high current draw and noise rather than as a hard speed limit.

Compensation is currently hand-tuned every 100 rad/s with a fitted function used on the robot. Automating this — ramping through the speeds while minimising phase current — is open work.

Open question

Could TorqueControlType::foc_current give better control? The robot currently uses voltage mode, bypassing the current sensors.

Gearbox corrosion

By default the gearbox on the FAAV4 is not waterproof at all, and it is normal for it to fill completely with water once submerged. This was considered acceptable: over more than a year of repeated use in water, including in Lake Geneva, a gearbox was opened and showed no sign of corrosion.

Then a newer robot, built to experiment with transitions, showed severe corrosion after only 3–4 uses.

The cause is believed to be a combination of improper lubrication after a cleaning event, and the absence of the shaft bearing, which allowed more water in through the front of the gearbox. When an old gearbox was cleaned for installation in the new robot, no proper lubricant was available, so motor oil was used. Its low viscosity is believed to have washed it away quickly, leaving the gears dry and starting corrosion.

Which parts actually rust

Each individual part was left in water for 12 hours.

Rusts Does not rust
Sun gear, planet gears, ring gear, planet shafts Screws, motor shaft, washers, body, circlip

Resolution

A few simple optional modifications to the structure and assembly process keep most of the water out. Combined with proper lubrication — a mix of CorrosionX and white lithium grease — this appears to have solved the issue. Several dives have since been performed with the same gearbox that had previously corroded, and it now runs smoothly. The practical procedure is in Session 10.

Housing material tradeoff

Foam Floatability Absorption Impact behaviour
Extruded polystyrene Best, low density Zero Brittle; little help damping landings
Closed-cell polyethylene Decent, relatively low density Light Compliant; usable as protection

A multi-material housing has never been built but would likely be better than either.
