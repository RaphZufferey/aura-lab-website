Over the semester your team builds a flapping aerial-aquatic vehicle: a robot that flies, lands on water, swims by flapping the same wings, and takes off again. This manual is the build side of the course. It is sequenced to the weekly modules, and each page covers exactly what you do in that module.

How this manual is organised

Build modules are cumulative and in order. Each one opens with what you are building that day, what to have ready before you arrive, and ends with a checkpoint a TA signs off.

Reference holds the material you consult rather than follow: the parts list, the firmware repositories, the PCB revisions, the CNC guide, and the design notes explaining why the robot is the way it is.

Architecture in one paragraph

One 4S battery (16.8 V max) feeds a custom PCB that steps VBAT down to 5 V to power the flight controller, which in turn generates a 3v3 rail for the sensors on that PCB. Battery power passes through a shunt for current sensing, then out to the ESC. The flight controller commands the ESC over serial. The ESC drives a brushless motor through a 23.04:1 gearbox, and a crank–linkage converts that rotation into wing flapping.

Versioning

This is version fall-2026. Corrections published during the semester are dated in the Changelog. If a step here contradicts what a TA tells you at the bench, the TA is right and this page is stale — report it so the next team does not lose time to it.
