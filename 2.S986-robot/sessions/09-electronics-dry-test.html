Session 9 — Flight controller, PCB and dry testing

Week of Nov 9 · Paired lecture: The future of actuation

What you build today

The custom PCB assembled and validated, the flight controller flashed and talking to the ESC, and the robot flapping in air under wireless control.

Before you arrive

Parts FAAV PCB v3.0, stencil, solder paste, XIAO ESP32S3, JST connectors, battery
Tools Hotplate, tweezers, multimeter, bench supply with current limit
Read This page and PCB revisions

Electronic architecture

One 4S (max 16.8 V) battery is connected to a custom PCB that steps VBAT down to 5 V to power the flight controller. The flight controller generates a 3v3 rail to power the sensors on the custom PCB. Battery power goes through a shunt for sensing on the custom PCB, then back out to power the ESC. The flight controller sends commands to the ESC over serial.

Flight controller

Firmware lives in FAAV-Flight-Controller (ESP32, FreeRTOS tasks). See Software for older variants.

Assembling the PCB

As of April 2026 the current board is v3.0: 4 layers, 21 × 28.5 mm, 34 components. The complete BOM is in the Manufacturing/Assembly folder of the FAAV-PCB-v3 repository.

The simplest way to assemble a FAAV PCB is with a stencil, solder paste applied with a cutter blade, then reflow on the hotplate.

Taking the PCB into operation

Once assembled, solder power wires for VBAT and GND at the input side of the board. Follow these steps before powering the PCB for the first time:

  1. Check that there is no short circuit between GND and 5V / 3.3V / VBAT.
  2. Connect a 15 V supply with a 100 mA current limit to the battery pads and check that it does not draw excessive current.
  3. Check with a multimeter that the buck regulator outputs 5 V from the 15 V input. The MCU should be on by then.

With the MCU powered, flash your test firmware and check the connection to all sensors. Table 1 gives the I²C addresses.

Verify:

Once all the hardware on the board is validated, solder the power wires at the output side of the board to the ESC, and the UART wires.

WarningTX to RX

The TX output of the PCB has to be connected to the RX pin of the ESC, and vice versa.

Connectors

The waterproof connector is the JST 8-pin 08R-JWPF-VSLE-D / 08T-JWPF-VSLE-D. All pins marked _b are used for balancing the battery during recharge. For use on the robot, connect at least Vbat and GND; to allow higher currents, connect Vbat_b and GND_b as well.

WarningSeal the unused cable slots

Do not forget to seal the back of the connector for the unused cable slots — this is a water ingress path straight into the electronics.

Dry testing

ImportantTo be written by the instructor

The dry-test protocol is course material, not in the guide. Needed: what the robot is mounted on while flapping in air, the flap frequency sweep, what is measured, the wireless control setup students reprogram to, and abort criteria.

NoteCheckpoint — TA sign-off

PCB validated against all four sensor checks, flight controller commanding the ESC over serial, and a supervised flapping run in air.
