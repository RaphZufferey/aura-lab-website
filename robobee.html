At a milligram scale, the Robobee employs combustion as a mean to escape the water surface. This project was developed jointly with the Harvard Microrobotics Lab in 2016-2017. At this scale, the physics at play are different and the water escape is dominated by surface tension. Thiseffectively prohibits the robot from “flying” off from the surface. To overcome this, the microrobotic bee of just 160 milligrams was adjusted with a small chamber capable of exploding hydrogen from the water through electrolysis. The combustion process produces only water and creates forces of 23000 times the weight of therobot, launching the robot into the air. This research was published in Science Robotics in 2017, showcasing the first ever micro robot capable of locomotion in air, in water and transitioning in-between.
